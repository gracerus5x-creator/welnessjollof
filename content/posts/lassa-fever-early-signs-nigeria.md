Lassa fever kills roughly **one in four** confirmed patients in Nigeria — and the
single biggest reason is that people reach hospital **too late**. It starts
looking exactly like malaria, so it gets waved off. This guide covers the early
signs, how it actually spreads, and the simple steps that protect your household.

## Why Lassa is so easy to miss

In its first days Lassa looks like ordinary malaria: fever, weakness, headache.
That resemblance is the danger — treated **early**, survival is far higher; late,
it becomes deadly. So the rule is simple: with the signs below, **get tested;
don't wait it out.**

## Early warning signs — don't dismiss them

See a doctor early if you notice:

- **Fever** that won't break, with weakness
- **Sore throat, headache, muscle and chest pain**
- **Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhoea**
- **Bleeding** from the gums, nose, or eyes
- **Facial swelling or confusion** — later, dangerous signs

## How Lassa spreads (this is what prevention targets)

The virus comes from the **multimammate rat**, shed in its **urine and
droppings**. People catch it mainly through **food and household surfaces
contaminated** by rats, by breathing in dust where droppings have dried, and — in
hospitals — through an infected person's body fluids. The incubation period is
about **one to three weeks**.

## Protect your home — five practical steps

1. **Store all food in sealed, rat-proof containers** — garri, rice, beans, everything.
2. **Never dry food outside** where rats can reach it.
3. **Block rat entry** — seal holes and clear bush around the house.
4. **Sweep damp, not dry** — dry sweeping throws contaminated dust into the air.
5. **Cover dustbins and keep the kitchen tight.**

## If you suspect Lassa fever

**Go to the nearest hospital immediately — do not self-medicate.** Nigeria's NCDC
runs a free emergency line: **6232**.

> Outbreak figures change weekly. For the latest confirmed case and death counts,
