Malaria is so common in Nigeria that many families treat it as routine — yet it
still kills, especially children under five and pregnant women. Nigeria carries
the **world's heaviest malaria burden**, roughly a quarter of all cases globally
(WHO). The good news: most malaria is **preventable at home**, with cheap, simple
habits. Here's exactly what works.

## First, how malaria actually spreads

Malaria is passed by the bite of an infected **female *Anopheles* mosquito**,
which bites mainly at **night — dusk to dawn**. It is **not** caught from food,
dirty water you drink, or "the sun." Because the mosquito bites at night and
breeds in still water, that's precisely where prevention wins.

## 6 ways to stop malaria at home

1. **Sleep under an insecticide-treated net (ITN) — every night.** This is the
   single most effective home defence, especially for children and pregnant
   women. Use it even when it's hot.
2. **Kill the breeding grounds — clear stagnant water.** Mosquitoes breed in
   still water: old tyres, buckets, empty containers, blocked gutters, discarded
   sachets. Empty, cover, or drain them weekly. No standing water, far fewer
   mosquitoes.
3. **Screen windows and doors**, and close them before dusk when mosquitoes come
   out.
4. **Clear bush and overgrowth** around the house where mosquitoes rest.
5. **Use repellents** on exposed skin in the evening, and consider indoor spraying.
6. **Protect the most vulnerable first** — pregnant women and under-fives should
   never sleep without a net. Pregnant women should also attend antenatal care,
   where prevention is part of the routine.

## The mistake that costs lives: "It's just malaria"

Not every fever is malaria — typhoid, and even **Lassa fever**, start the same
way. **Test before you treat.** A quick blood test (RDT or microscopy) at a
pharmacy, clinic, or lab confirms it. Treating "malaria" blindly means you may
be swallowing the wrong drug — or a **fake antimalarial** — while the real
illness gets worse.

And if a fever comes with **bleeding, confusion, or severe weakness**, or won't
break with proper treatment, go to a hospital immediately — that is not ordinary
malaria.

## Prompt treatment matters

If a test confirms malaria, start the **full, correct course** of treatment your
healthcare provider prescribes — and finish it, even when you feel better.
Half-doses fuel drug resistance. Never self-prescribe; ask your pharmacist or
doctor.

> **A word on children:** newer WHO-recommended childhood malaria vaccines are
> being rolled out in high-burden regions. Ask your clinic whether your child is
> eligible — it adds to, but does not replace, sleeping under a net.

## The bottom line

Malaria in Nigeria is beatable at home: **net every night, no standing water,
test before you treat.** Simple habits, repeated, protect your whole family.
